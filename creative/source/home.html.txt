Story identifiers and source attribution follow the material into the script and the review package.</p></article><article><span class="tag">02 / Check before voicing</span><h3>Make editorial checks actionable.</h3><p>Script QA checks attribution, sensitive reporting and copied source passages. Rejected scripts or unapproved sources stop voice generation. Captions take their words from the authored script; speech recognition supplies timing.</p></article><article><span class="tag">03 / Keep human judgment</span><h3>Review the episode as a whole.</h3><p>The operator receives the script, audio, captions and review checklist together. Improvements move through experiments, review and an approval gate.</p></article></div><p class="fine" style="margin-top:24px">The current prototype packages audio and captions for human review. Its wider production design extends to video and YouTube distribution.</p></section>
<section class="section section-anchor" id="exploration"><span class="eyebrow">Musical exploration &amp; composition</span><h2>Before the first note settles.</h2><p class="intro" style="margin-top:22px">Classical interests and experimental approaches meet in detailed writing about texture, pacing and the role of an instrument.</p><div class="process-grid"><article><span class="tag">01 / Instrument roles</span><h3>What changes when the instrument changes?</h3><p>A đàn bầu can be explored as a rhythmic voice, a melodic reply or a long transition. The question is what it contributes to the composition.</p><p style="margin-top:16px">An earlier Same Wet Reeds arrangement asks for spare đàn bầu replies beside fingerpicked guitar. Hud mot hud explores it against bass and woodwinds.</p><a class="text-link" href="/creative/same-wet-reeds/">Explore Same Wet Reeds →</a></article><article><span class="tag">02 / The introduction</span><h3>Sometimes an intro takes pages.</h3><p>Fredrik describes writing one to three A4 pages to develop an introduction: when each voice enters, which instruments remain silent, and how the opening moves.</p><p style="margin-top:16px">Awaken Wired (Chalumeau, Sul) is his selected example for this part of the collection.</p><a class="text-link" href="#awaken">About the intro example →</a></article><article><span class="tag">03 / Sound development</span><h3>Turn a feeling into musical choices.</h3><p>The preparation for Hud mot hud connects a tactile sound idea to register, timing, vocal proximity, stereo width and the order of sections.</p><p style="margin-top:16px">The brief, the code and the seed let you follow different stages of that process.</p><a class="text-link" href="/creative/hud-mot-hud/">Follow the process →</a></article></div></section>
<section class="section section-anchor" id="learning"><div class="paper"><div class="split"><div><span class="eyebrow">Learn through music</span><h2>A rap battle.<br>A conceptual disagreement.</h2><p style="margin-top:24px"><em>Superposition Baby, more Concepts than You got Cells</em> turns an argument about weights, neurons and features into competing voices, repetition and a final joke.</p><p style="margin-top:16px">Listen for the correction: the opening voice confidently mixes things up. The answer is part of the song.</p><div class="actions"><a class="button light" href="https://suno.com/song/791d21b2-c908-4a1e-8861-ff300ae66461" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Listen on Suno ↗</a></div><p class="fine" style="margin-top:18px">Artist name: Ragnar Bratten · Educational experiment</p></div><div><h3 style="margin-bottom:16px">Open a line. Follow the idea.</h3><div class="learning-terms"><details><summary>“Weights in the file, features in the flow”</summary><p class="lyric">A compact hook, with a useful distinction.</p><p>Weights are stored model parameters. Activations are values computed for an input. Interpretability research studies recurring patterns in those activations as features. The weights shape how those patterns arise; the hook simplifies that relationship.</p></details><details><summary>“More concepts than you got cells”</summary><p class="lyric">Superposition as a musical image.</p><p>In the cited toy-model research, a network can represent more features than it has dimensions by using overlapping directions. Applying this picture to larger models requires care; a feature is not simply one neuron with one fixed meaning.</p></details><details><summary>Why does the first verse get it wrong?</summary><p class="lyric">The disagreement is the teaching device.</p><p>The song first presents a misconception, then challenges it. The exchange gives the listener both the misconception and the answer, using rhythm and repetition to make the distinction memorable.</p></details></div><div class="source-links"><a href="https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Research: Toy Models of Superposition ↗</a><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/mapping-mind-language-model" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Research: Mapping the mind ↗</a></div></div></div></div></section>
<section class="section section-anchor" id="awaken"><div class="split"><div><span class="eyebrow">Composition notebook / Supplied original instructions</span><h2>Awaken Wired<br><span style="font:italic 30px Georgia,serif;color:var(--warm)">(Chalumeau, Sul)</span></h2><p class="artist">Artist name: Ragnar Bratten</p><div class="work-facts"><div><b>36 bars</b>The introduction alone</div><div><b>7/8 · 2+2+3</b>Written metric design</div><div><b>Five phases</b>From ppp to fff</div></div></div><div><p class="intro">An introduction composed as a sequence of changing states: the room breathes, a mechanical pulse takes hold, a light clicks on, and a chamber ensemble gathers until it is cut away.</p><p class="muted" style="margin-top:20px">Fredrik’s supplied instructions connect everyday sounds to a tightly specified musical form. Cloth, breath, wire hangers and radiator ticks become part of the written arrangement alongside prepared guitar, harmonium, bass clarinet, flute and strings.</p><div class="actions"><a class="button" href="https://suno.com/song/03f72637-ce31-4334-8eee-0e5d761414a3" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Listen on Suno ↗</a></div></div></div>
<div class="section-head" style="margin-top:44px"><h3>Five phases of one opening</h3><p class="fine">The written plan: 116 BPM, 7/8 grouped 2+2+3.</p></div>
<div class="phase-table-wrap"><table class="phase-table"><thead><tr><th scope="col">Bars / Phase</th><th scope="col">Dramatic movement</th><th scope="col">What the instructions ask for</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">1–4 / 1A</th><td><strong>The room becomes audible</strong></td><td>Granular fabric and breath textures, small metal sounds and a very quiet, dry, widely separated stereo space.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">5–12 / 1B</th><td><strong>A rhythmic motor takes hold</strong></td><td>Prepared acoustic guitar repeats D–A–E–A–C–A–G. Radiator ticks supply metric accents while the fabric layer continues.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">13–20 / 2A</th><td><strong>A switch triggers a new foundation</strong></td><td>A central lamp-switch click cues a D/A harmonium drone and electrical hum. Bass clarinet enters in the chalumeau register.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">21–28 / 2B</th><td><strong>The chamber texture tightens</strong></td><td>Viola and cello use sul ponticello tremolos, harmonics and col legno gestures. Alto flute adds interlocking fragments above the continuing drone.</td></tr>

<div class="process-grid"><article><span class="tag">Continuity</span><h3>Three layers keep the room present.</h3><p>The granular fabric-and-breath texture, the prepared-guitar motor with radiator accents, and the harmonium/hum foundation are introduced separately. They are asked to persist through the larger ensemble and remain when it drops away.</p></article><article><span class="tag">Instrumental writing</span><h3>Technique carries the image.</h3><p>Chalumeau register, sul ponticello, col legno and portamento give the instructions a specific palette. These choices connect the desired physical sensation to an instrumental action.</p></article><article><span class="tag">Dramaturgy</span><h3>The final cut changes what came before.</h3><p>The climax is planned together with its disappearance. Returning to the bare room makes the small, early textures part of the ending of the introduction.</p></article></div>
