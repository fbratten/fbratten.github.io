Capability Boundary Expander

Complete the responsibility.
Preserve the boundary.

CBE asks what an already defined capability still needs to perform its own responsibility. It separates legitimate internal expansion from work owned by neighboring capabilities, then makes the boundary and handoffs explicit.

One bounded analysis

Expand from a demonstrated gap

A useful nearby feature is not automatically part of the target's responsibility. Every candidate must trace to an internal gap and survive relevance, ownership and identity checks.

Start with

  • A named target and its purpose.
  • Current responsibilities and a locked responsibility invariant.
  • Known peers, constraints and supporting evidence.

Unknown ownership stays unresolved until evidence or an architectural decision can settle it.

Return

  • Internal gaps and classified candidates.
  • Retained responsibilities and their boundaries.
  • Explicit handoff contracts and rejected scope.
  • Residual uncertainty and verification findings.
  1. Lock the target's responsibility and normalize what it already owns.
  2. Find internal gaps; derive candidate extensions from those gaps.
  3. Check target relevance and substantive overlap with peers.
  4. Classify, split mixed responsibilities and record handoffs.
  5. Audit completeness, preserved responsibilities, uncertainty and target identity.
Four verdicts, four different consequences
VerdictUse whenDesign result
KEEPThe responsibility is needed and belongs to the target.Add it to the target's design.
SPLITThe candidate mixes independently meaningful responsibilities.Classify the resulting parts separately.
DELEGATEThe target needs the result, but a peer owns the operation.Define a handoff and expected return.
REJECTThe candidate does not close an internal gap or changes target identity.Record the reason for excluding it.

UNRESOLVED records insufficient evidence or an ownership question. It is not a fifth acceptance verdict. Using a peer's output does not mean owning that peer's operation.

Shared example with CBE-IX

A document ingestion pipeline

Responsibility invariant: accept documents and produce normalized ingestible records. Preserve the existing steps: accept, extract, normalize and submit downstream. The Semantic Indexer owns embeddings; the Retention Policy Service owns retention decisions; the Malware Scanner owns malicious-content detection.

Retained boundary

Owns: attaching the supplied retention classification to a normalized record.

Does not own: defining retention policy or deciding how long records must be retained.

Input: normalized record plus a classification returned by the policy service.

Output: enriched record, or an explicit unresolved classification.

Handoff contract

Trigger: a record needs retention classification.

Destination: Retention Policy Service.

Target supplies: the agreed record metadata.

Destination owns: the policy decision.

Expected return: classification or unresolved result. The pipeline attaches the return or preserves the unresolved state.

The candidate verdict changes the design representation. It does not edit the pipeline, call the peer or authorize deployment.

Where CBE fits

Discover before expanding

Capability-Gap and CSR establish what exists and where a need is already covered. CBE then examines the selected target's internal completeness. SORR can resolve ambiguous identities and responsibility claims.

Control the analysis

Dialogue Lifecycle controls transitions. 5PP Variation A structures the scoped work and audit. AICS can represent the resulting requirements and decisions. Analysis authorization and implementation authorization remain separate.

When to use CBE-IX

Use CBE-IX when an accepted extension may expose further legitimate internal gaps. It adds an iteration ledger, a frontier of unprocessed work and explicit convergence checks.
